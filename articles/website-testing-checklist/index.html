Website testing checklist: what to check before you launch

By SoftScout. Published 24 November 2026.

In short

Before launch, test every page and form the way a new visitor would: on a phone and a computer, in at least two browsers, with real tasks such as signing up or buying. Check that text, prices and help pages match what is on screen, that forms really deliver, and that the site is fast and readable. Record each problem with a screenshot and the steps to repeat it.

How to use this checklist

Go through it with real tasks, not by clicking around. Pick the three or four things a visitor most often comes to do (find a product, ask for a quote, sign up, pay) and complete each one from start to finish. Do it once on a phone and once on a computer.

1. Pages and navigation

  • Every menu item opens the right page, on phone and computer.
  • No broken links (pages that show an error or "not found").
  • Unknown addresses show a helpful "page not found" page with a way back.
  • The logo returns to the home page.
  • Page titles in the browser tab are different for each page and describe it.

2. Forms and sign-up

  • Each form sends, and the message actually arrives where it should (check the inbox, not just the "thank you" screen).
  • Required fields are marked, and error messages say what to fix.
  • Phone numbers, emails and names in your language are accepted.
  • Sign-up, log-in and "forgot password" work end to end, including the email they send.
  • Spam protection does not block real people.

3. Checkout and payment (if you sell online)

  • The price on the product page matches the cart and the final total.
  • Discounts, delivery charges and taxes are added correctly.
  • The payment step works on iPhone and Android, not only on a computer.
  • The confirmation page and email show the right order details.

4. Phones, tablets and browsers

  • No sideways scrolling on a phone; text is readable without zooming.
  • Buttons are big enough to tap and are not hidden behind cookie banners or chat bubbles.
  • The site works in Chrome, Safari and at least one other browser.
  • Images and videos load and fit the screen.

5. Content accuracy

  • Product names, prices, phone numbers and addresses are correct and the same everywhere.
  • Help pages and guides use the same button and menu names as the live screens.
  • No placeholder text, test data or old offers left on the site.
  • Claims such as "fastest" or "award-winning" can be backed up.
  • Both language versions say the same thing, if your site has two languages.

6. Speed and accessibility basics

  • Main pages open in a few seconds on a mobile connection.
  • Images have short descriptions (alt text) that say what they show.
  • Text has enough contrast against the background.
  • You can move through the page with the keyboard and see where you are.

7. Search, AI assistants and tracking

  • Each page has a clear title and a one-sentence description.
  • A sitemap exists and robots.txt does not block pages you want found.
  • Your hosting or security service is not blocking the search and AI crawlers you want.
  • Analytics records visits once, not twice, and ignores your own test visits if possible.

8. After launch

  • Repeat the main tasks on the live address, not only on the test site.
  • Check that "www" and the plain address both work and that the padlock (HTTPS) shows.
  • Send a test enquiry through every contact route and confirm it arrives.
  • Check again after every big change; problems often come back with updates.

How to record what you find

Write one line per problem with: the page, what you did, what you expected, what happened, a screenshot, the device and browser, and how serious it is. That way whoever fixes it does not need to ask you anything. Our guide on how to write a bug report has a template.

Questions and answers

How long does website testing take?

For a small business site of 5 to 15 pages, a careful pass on phone and computer usually takes one to two working days, including writing up the findings. Shops and apps with log-ins take longer because each flow has to be completed end to end.

Can I test my own website?

Yes, and you should. But people who built a site tend to follow the paths they know. A fresh tester, or a friend who has never seen it, finds different problems.

What should be fixed first?

Anything that stops a visitor from completing the main task: broken forms, payment errors, wrong prices and wrong contact details. Cosmetic issues can wait.
